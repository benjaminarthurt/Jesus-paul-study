# Jesus–Paul comparison method: the seven undisputed letters

This pilot asks what Jesus is represented as teaching in the four canonical Gospels and what **Paul writes in seven generally undisputed letters**: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians and Philemon. It does not assume the Gospel narratives are verbatim recordings of the historical Jesus or that their dates and authorships are uncontroversial. It does not assume that every later writing attributed to Paul is his personal letter.

## Source and speaker levels

- **Gospel witness**: a passage within Matthew, Mark, Luke or John, sometimes paired with a selected speech-bearing span; the span can contain narration or interlocutor speech.
- **Direct Pauline witness**: a selected passage in one of the seven generally undisputed letters, marked `paul-undisputed`; the label refers to the overall letter's broadly accepted authorship rather than proving every reading original.
- **Contextual witness**: Acts, a disputed Pauline letter, or another passage needed to see the broader debate. It receives an explicit rationale and must never be displayed as Paul speaking directly.
- **Primary edition**: public-domain World English Bible Protestant edition. The site links to the individual chapters and uses original editorial paraphrases, not a copyrighted Bible translation's verses.

The site records what each text **actually addresses**, what it **shares**, how its immediate audience, rhetorical setting or institutional context **differs**, the strongest **counterreading**, and at least one **limitation**.

## Classification burden

| Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Agreement | Overlapping guidance without claiming identical wording or independent attestation |
| Different emphasis | Different applications, audiences or genre, without incompatible claims |
| Extension | A later elaboration compatible with an earlier teaching, to be evaluated historically |
| Apparent tension | Plausible incompatible readings exist but credible contextual or language alternatives remain |
| Interpretation-dependent | Significance depends on disputed translation, textual or historical interpretation |
| Insufficient evidence | The cited passages do not justify a strong conclusion |
| Direct contradiction | **Only** if two proposition-level claims about the same subject, scope and circumstances remain mutually incompatible after relevant counterreadings; this needs explicit extra evidence fields and independent review |

**No pilot record currently asserts an established direct contradiction.** That is not a claim that no contradictions exist. It means the evidence burden has not yet been met.

## Common traps and safeguards

1. Do not quote the legal expert's answer in Luke 10:25–28 as direct words of Jesus; compare it as dialogue.
2. Do not infer that 1 Corinthians 7's reference to the Lord must use the exact surviving Mark/Matthew/Luke wording.
3. Do not treat John 7:53–8:11 or Mark 16:9–20 as manuscript-secure Gospel sayings without textual qualification.
4. Do not silently promote 1 Timothy, Titus or Acts to a verified Pauline authorial voice.
5. Do not present Philemon as an explicit recorded abolition of legal slavery or approval of slavery by implication alone.
6. Do not turn Romans 13 into a modern political platform or read Jesus' coin answer as a comprehensive church–state constitution.
7. Distinguish love toward enemies from the separate question of divine judgment or public force.
8. Note manuscript variation, audience, chronology and different rhetorical purposes before declaring contradiction.
