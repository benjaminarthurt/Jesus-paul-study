# Gospel comparison pilot — research and editorial review log

**November 18, 2026 · Draft research.**

## What was verified structurally

The 16 dossiers contain 51 witness links selected from the previously committed four-Gospel literary catalogs. IDs and same-Gospel parentage are validated automatically; no dossier asserts four independent historical eyewitness accounts. Seven studies compare presentations in all four canonical Gospels.

## Sources and their limits

- [John S. Kloppenborg, *The History and Prospects of the Synoptic Problem*](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/45760/chapter-abstract/398723290): Oxford University Press. **Publisher abstract only** was available; no claim about a particular pericope is drawn from inaccessible full text.
- [Felix Just, *Contrasts between John and the Synoptics*](https://ww.catholic-resources.org/John/Synoptic-Differences.htm): accessible comparative overview consulted for differences in anointing, temple chronology, feeding, and Johannine meal scenes. This is an educational synthesis, not a critical edition.
- [USCCB, *Gospel according to John: Introduction*](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0): accessible scholarly introduction consulted for the fourth Gospel's distinct editorial presentation. It is a Roman Catholic commentary perspective, not a universally accepted historical theory.
- [World English Bible Protestant edition](https://ebible.org/engwebp/): every witness links externally to the primary source chapter. A full manual verse-by-verse textual audit remains pending.

## High-risk claims explicitly guarded

- Luke 10:25–28 includes commandments spoken by a legal expert, so the two highest commandments are not automatically quoted verbatim from Jesus in Luke.
- Luke 7's anointing can be compared with Bethany but is marked `distinct-scene`, not a secure same-event parallel.
- John's temple action is placed near the beginning of the ministry, unlike the Synoptic Jerusalem sequence; two-event and literary relocation theories remain open.
- John's footwashing is a related enacted teaching, not the same words as the Synoptic service saying or Passover cup/bread discourse.
- The manuscript-disputed longer Mark ending is not a parallel to the earliest ending account, and John 7:53–8:11 is not assigned to a core saying.
- Luke 22's textual variation remains visible in the final-meal dossier.
- The widow's gift is not turned into a universal instruction that poor people donate their final money.
