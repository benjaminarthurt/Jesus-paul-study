# Project charter

## Core question

If contemporary Christianity is examined primarily against the teachings and actions attributed to Jesus in the canonical Gospels, which beliefs and practices can be traced to those accounts, which to Paul or other early Christian writers, and which to later interpretive traditions?

## Goals

- Produce a complete, traceable index of relevant New Testament teachings, with Hebrew Bible and Second Temple Jewish context.
- Compare Jesus and Paul on specific ethical and theological topics, documenting agreements as well as apparent contradictions.
- Analyze how competing Christian traditions apply those writings to modern social, political, and moral issues.
- Publish accessible research pages and machine-readable references through a searchable website.

## Non-goals

- Assume in advance that Jesus and Paul always disagree, or that all New Testament authors agree.
- Treat Gospel dialogue as independently verified verbatim transcripts of the historical Jesus.
- Treat modern political categories as if they existed in the first century.
- Claim that silence on a contemporary policy proves endorsement or opposition.
- Present a disputed authorship position as certain.

## Audience and editorial stance

Written for interested lay readers, practicing Christians, skeptics, and researchers. The project transparently uses Jesus' recorded teachings as its primary comparative frame while evaluating specific claims with historical and textual evidence.

Research conclusions are revisable and must be supported with documented passages, sources, and counterarguments.
