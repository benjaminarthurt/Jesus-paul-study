# Wealth and poverty: source review log

**Date:** 2026-11-17 — initial comparative research, status `draft`.

## Primary texts inspected
- WEB Protestant 66-book edition: Mark 10 and 12; Luke 6, 12, 16, 18, 19; Matthew 6, 19, 25; 1 Corinthians 16; 2 Corinthians 8–9; Romans 12 and 15; Galatians 2; Acts 4; 1 Timothy 6.
- The Bible records are linked to complete source chapters. Summaries are original paraphrases rather than reproduced translations.
- 1 Timothy is intentionally a separate, **disputed Pauline** context source; Acts is a later narrative, not first-person Paul.

## Secondary works and precise access
- **Barclay (2023)**, *Rich Poverty*, `New Testament Studies` 69(3), 243–257. Open full article inspected, especially the Macedonian, Corinthian and conclusions sections. Interprets giving as a social economy of grace. Notes harms from pressuring impoverished people to donate.
- **Welborn (2013)**, *That There May Be Equality*, `New Testament Studies` 59(1), 73–90. **Publisher abstract only.** Do not assert contents of its inaccessible pages.
- **Ndekha (2020)**, Luke 6, `HTS Teologiese Studies` 76(4), a6065. Full article inspected; panegyric reading with a specifically Malawian social application.
- **van Zyl (2013)**, Matthew 25, `Acta Theologica` 33 suppl. 17. Full online article inspected; treats universal and ecclesiological readings of the "least".
- **Shively (2012)**, Luther Seminary *Working Preacher* Mark 10 interpretation. Full online commentary inspected. Type is pastoral commentary, not a peer-reviewed journal article.
- **Kubiś (2015)**, Mark 12 widow, `The Biblical Annals` 3(2), 339–381. **Publisher abstract only.** Argues for exemplary self-denial while acknowledging a negative reading. Do not cite unseen article pages.

## Methodological decisions
1. A narrative report of Zacchaeus's half-gift is not a general numerical rule.
2. Mark's widow is not commanded by Jesus to give her last funds; historical and literary interpretations differ.
3. Paul's 2 Corinthians 8–9 collection is an identifiable charitable project and should not be called a modern tax policy.
4. Matthew 25's "least of these" has serious competing interpretative identifications.
5. Gospel and Pauline source groups are distinguished from Acts and disputed Pastoral Epistles.
6. All claims have a counterreading and stated limitation.
