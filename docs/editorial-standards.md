# Editorial and citation standards

## Required sections for substantial studies

1. Question and scope
2. Primary passages with full references and contextual summaries
3. Historical and literary analysis
4. Agreement and tension analysis
5. Strongest counterreading / possible reconciliation
6. Modern relevance and limits of application
7. Provisional conclusion and certainty limits
8. Bibliography and revision notes

## Source integrity

Use explicit primary references (book, chapter, verses, translation). Academic claims need author, work, year, pages or stable location, and any relevant edition. Prefer peer-reviewed scholarship and established critical commentaries; do not misrepresent denominational views as scholarly consensus.

Paraphrases must be original, labeled as summaries rather than quotation. Quotations must be accurate and legally reusable. Maintain source IDs so citations can be resolved and audited.

## Review checklist

- Have we included the wider paragraph or pericope?
- Have we tested cross-Gospel differences and competing passages?
- Is the writer/speaker correctly attributed?
- Have we addressed genuine text-critical concerns?
- Is the proposed comparison stronger than mere difference of emphasis?
- Is a modern conclusion explicitly distinguished from an ancient instruction?
- Would a careful critic consider the counterargument fairly presented?

Do not publish any draft as a settled theological or historical conclusion.
