# Jesus–Paul comparisons — initial review log

**November 18, 2026 — draft structural and interpretive pilot.** No claim was independently peer reviewed.

## Witness coverage and source limits

- **11 draft cross-corpus dossiers**: 24 Gospel literary witnesses and 26 direct Pauline passage witnesses, across all **seven** generally undisputed letters.
- **Nine new selected Pauline passages**: Romans 12:14–21 and 13:1–7; 1 Corinthians 7:17–24 and 13:1–13; Philippians 2:1–11 and 4:4–7; 1 Thessalonians 4:9–12 and 5:12–22; Philemon 1:8–21.
- All nine have separate stable research IDs, trusted WEBP source-chapter URLs, `paul-undisputed` labels and reciprocal topic registration.
- The primary wording was checked selectively in the public WEB/Webp ecosystem for Romans 12–13, Philippians 2, 1 Thessalonians 4 and Philemon. **Not** a complete textual collation of nine passages or the entire Pauline corpus.
- Existing scholarly-source records are reused for Law, wealth and women. In particular, the Matthew Thiessen and L. L. Welborn references remain **publisher-abstract-only** and cannot support uninspected arguments.

## Editorial observations and safeguards

- Romans 12's blessing and nonretaliation overlap substantially with Matthew 5/Luke 6; divine vengeance language must not be equated with human revenge.
- Matthew 5 and Galatians present an apparent tension about Torah while Romans/Galatians also affirm neighbor-love. Paul is not flatly labeled an opponent of the whole Law.
- 1 Corinthians 7 differentiates Lord-attributed instructions from Paul's additional guidance; its context of mixed-belief households matters.
- Named female coworkers in Romans 16/Philippians 4 and women's prayer/prophecy in 1 Corinthians 11 remain distinct from contested 1 Corinthians 14 and the disputed-authorship Pastoral letters.
- Jesus' portrayal of authority, Paul's appeal to Philemon, and Romans 13's political instruction have different audiences and rhetorical genres.
- Galatians 3, Philemon and 1 Corinthians 7 support analysis of the status of enslaved believers; their presence does not amount to an explicit documented manumission directive.
- Matthew 6 and Luke 12 counsel against worry; 1 Thessalonians 4 encourages work. These are not labeled an established logical contradiction.
- **No case labeled direct contradiction without common-scope proposition-level analysis.** This retains open research questions and refuses premature harmonization as well as premature antagonism.
