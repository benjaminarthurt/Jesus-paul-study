## Evidence inspected

- Matthew 5:17–48 and Mark 7; Galatians 2–5 and Romans 3, 7, 8, 10, 13; Acts 15 — World English Bible Protestant edition (engwebp), with original paraphrase rather than copied text.
- Matthew V. Novenson, *Law in the New Testament* (2023), St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology. Full online overview, including sections comparing Matthew and Pauline letters.
- Bradley M. Trout (2015), Matthew 5:17. Peer-reviewed article with a specific kingdom/fulfillment reading; author interpretation distinguished from consensus.
- Logan Williams (2024), Mark 7:18–19 in *New Testament Studies*. A critical grammatical counterreading, not assumed to invalidate every existing translation.
- Matthew Thiessen (2016), *Paul and the Gentile Problem*. **Publisher abstract only** inspected. No claims about inaccessible chapters or exact pages.
- American Bible Society, *The Council in Jerusalem*. Accessible account comparing Acts 15 and Galatians 2; not a definitive specialist adjudication.

## Findings framed cautiously

Matthew's Gospel presents Jesus as upholding the Law while using fulfillment language; Paul's letters can describe both a transformed relationship to Law and its ongoing ethical value. Galatians directly disputes Torah-based Gentile inclusion. Differences between audience, genre, and literary chronology are necessary to any precise contradiction claim.
