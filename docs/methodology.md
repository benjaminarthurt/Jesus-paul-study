# Research methodology — version 0.1

## 1. Corpus and source hierarchy

1. **Synoptic Gospel accounts:** Mark, Matthew, Luke. Compare literary overlap, differences, implied audience, and narrative setting.
2. **John:** analyze distinctive sayings and portrayal of Jesus separately; do not collapse Johannine and Synoptic language into a single unqualified historical reconstruction.
3. **Undisputed Pauline letters:** Romans, 1 Corinthians, 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon.
4. **Pauline authorship debated:** Ephesians, Colossians, 2 Thessalonians, 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy, Titus. Debates vary by letter.
5. **Acts:** a narrative source about Paul and earliest Christianity, not an uncritical substitute for Paul's own letters.
6. **Other New Testament witnesses:** Hebrews, James, Petrine letters, Johannine letters, Jude, Revelation.
7. **Hebrew Bible, Second Temple Jewish sources, subsequent Christian interpretation:** contextual evidence, with genre and chronology explicit.

## 2. Claims have four distinct layers

- **Text:** what the passage actually says in context, including ambiguity and textual variants.
- **Historical reading:** a scholarly inference about author, audience, circumstances, and possible original meaning.
- **Theological reading:** how the passage is understood within a particular interpretive tradition.
- **Present-day application:** a reasoned extension to a modern concern, explicitly marked when indirect.

Avoid laundering a present-day application into a direct quotation or an ancient author's explicit position.

## 3. Comparison classifications

- **agreement:** substantive consistency or mutual reinforcement.
- **different-emphasis:** different priority, rhetoric, or addressed audience without demonstrated incompatibility.
- **extension:** a response to circumstances beyond those addressed in the compared text.
- **apparent-tension:** potentially inconsistent teachings requiring adjudication.
- **direct-contradiction:** two appropriately contextualized claims that cannot both hold under a specified interpretation; unusually high evidentiary threshold.
- **insufficient-evidence:** silence, uncertain attribution, or inadequate basis to decide.

Classification is provisional until source checking. Explicitly state whether a conclusion depends on disputed chronology, composition, translation, or authorship.

## 4. Research workflow for every passage

1. Identify literary unit, genre, speaker, audience, manuscript context, and parallel passages.
2. Record the relevant reference by stable ID; do not duplicate long excerpts.
3. Note significant translation choices and relevant Greek or Hebrew terms where necessary.
4. Record historically credible interpretations and their supporting evidence.
5. Identify the strongest plausible counterreading, not a caricature.
6. Compare across writings, using the classification scale above.
7. Distinguish the inference required for modern application.
8. Attach verifiable full bibliographic citations and a correction history.
9. Change research status only after an independent source check.

## 5. Textual and authorship cautions

Illustrations requiring notes when relevant: John 7:53–8:11; Mark 16:9–20; the position/textual status of 1 Corinthians 14:34–35; interpretive debate over women named in Romans 16. These issues must be assessed individually, not treated as identical.

The earliest surviving *written* Pauline letters predate the surviving written Gospels, but that does not imply Paul's ministry or teachings predate Jesus' life. Dating claims must be sourced.

## 6. Counterexample search

Before publishing a topic, deliberately search for passages contradicting the proposed thesis, cross-Gospel qualifications, and Pauline passages that align with Jesus. Compare both friendly and critical scholarship.

## 7. Status definitions

- `draft`: outline or preliminary analysis; not yet fully sourced.
- `source-checked`: every major claim tied to checked primary text and scholarly literature; disagreements represented.
- `reviewed`: source-checked plus a separate editorial review of alternative readings, citations, and modern applications.

Never equate a polished page with verified research.
