# Luke 10:1–37 — speech and parallel audit

**Issue:** #2 · **Status:** draft; not independently source-checked

## Unit-by-unit speech mapping

| Reference | Narrative speaker | Speech attribution / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:1 | Narrator | Appointment and sending of disciples, **not** direct speech by Jesus. Number of disciples is textually disputed (seventy / seventy-two). |
| 10:2–12 | Jesus | Direct commissioning discourse, with instructions on prayer, travel, hospitality, healing, proclamation and rejection. |
| 10:13–15 | Jesus | Woes to Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum, continuous speech following the commission. |
| 10:16 | Jesus | Saying about hearing or rejecting the emissaries; continuous speech. |
| 10:17 | Narrator / returning disciples | Return report; **not** Jesus' words. The seventy / seventy-two variation recurs. |
| 10:18–20 | Jesus | Reply to returning disciples, including Satan imagery, authority and the admonition to rejoice over names written in heaven. |
| 10:21 | Narrator introduces Jesus' rejoicing and thanksgiving | Narrative introduction followed by Jesus' prayer of thanksgiving to the Father. |
| 10:22 | Jesus (in the transmitted narrative) | Saying about Father/Son knowledge and revelation. Some editions differ in paragraph presentation, not necessarily in speaker attribution. |
| 10:23–24 | Narrator's aside, then Jesus | He turns privately to disciples and pronounces a blessing on those seeing what they see. |

## Synoptic parallel candidates requiring verse-level verification

- **Matthew 9:37–38:** harvest/laborers prayer alongside Luke 10:2.
- **Matthew 10:5–15 and Mark 6:7–13:** mission instructions, hospitality, and rejection; mission groups and narrative settings are not identical.
- **Matthew 11:20–24:** woes against Galilean towns parallel Luke 10:13–15.
- **Matthew 10:40 and Luke 9:48:** receiving or rejecting the messenger; compare wording and referents before treating as identical.
- **Matthew 11:25–27:** thanksgiving and Father/Son revelation parallels Luke 10:21–22.
- **Matthew 13:16–17:** blessing of seeing/hearing parallels Luke 10:23–24.

These are candidate parallels, not assertions of verbatim equivalence, common chronology, or literary dependence.

## Interpretive cautions

1. **Mission scale:** The seventy/seventy-two variant must remain visible; do not infer a settled historical number from a single translation.
2. **Mission audiences:** Matthew 10's restricted mission setting cannot automatically be assigned to Luke 10; preserve each evangelist's framing.
3. **Commission and judgment:** Distinguish Jesus' narrated words from the evangelist's placement and editorial arrangement.
4. **Revelation language:** Matthew 11:25–27 and Luke 10:21–22 show striking similarity, but their literary contexts differ; do not turn that into an unsupported reconstruction of a common source.
5. **Jesus–Paul comparisons:** Compare thematic content with Paul's mission practice only after differentiating narrative instructions for a specific mission from universal ethical requirements.

## The lawyer, the Samaritan and Jesus in Luke 10:25–37

In the two questions about the neighbor the same Greek word, **πλησίον**, occurs with a different point of reference. In 29 the lawyer asks who is his neighbor. In 36 Jesus asks which of the three seems to have become a neighbor to the injured man. **γεγονέναι** gives the second question its retrospective force. The lawyer must now judge the conduct of the travelers. The ensuing instruction asks him to act on his own answer. The lawyer voices the first part of 37, and Luke explicitly names Jesus as the speaker of the second. The Samaritan's promise in 35 is dialogue within Jesus' story. Recording it as a promise by Jesus to pay an innkeeper would change both speaker and recipient. The displayed Greek edition notes differences over a connective at the opening of 36 and before Jesus' final speech in 37. The alternatives leave the question, the lawyer's answer and Jesus' closing instruction distinguishable. These notes identify differences among editions. A judgment about particular manuscript witnesses would require further evidence.

In Mark the scribe asks which command ranks first. Jesus answers, the scribe compares love with offerings, and Jesus responds to him. In Matthew a lawyer tests Jesus, who gives both commands and comments on their relation to the Law and Prophets. In Luke the lawyer gives both commands and Jesus endorses his answer. The shared scriptural content warrants comparison of those exchanges. Extending the parallel through the end of the Samaritan story would imply a matching narrative absent from these passages in Mark and Matthew.
