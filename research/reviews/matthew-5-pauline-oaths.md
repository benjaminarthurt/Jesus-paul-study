# Matthew 5 and Pauline divine-witness appeals

## Scope and provisional conclusion

Matthew 5:33–37 prohibits swearing and demands reliable speech. Galatians 1:20 and 2 Corinthians 1:23 invoke divine testimony in support of Paul's assertions. The initial classification is revised to apparent tension, not direct contradiction. This is a draft review based on selected scholarly extracts, not complete edition collation.

## Claim checks

- Matthew's prohibition is broader in its wording than a prohibition of perjury alone. Whether it covers every solemn attestation remains disputed.
- Galatians 1:20 affirms the truthfulness of Paul's account of his contacts with Jerusalem. Hardin interprets the affirmation as an oath emphasizing the narrative, without requiring a reconstruction of opponents' accusations.
- In 2 Corinthians 1:23 Paul invokes God in explaining his decision not to revisit Corinth. Hardin reads this as an assurance of concern for the community.
- Novenson distinguishes the divine-witness idiom from self-imprecatory oaths: divine testimony supports the speaker's character rather than invoking punishment for default. His five examples include 2 Corinthians 1:23 but not Galatians 1:20. His argument must not be automatically extended to the latter.
- A distinction from self-imprecatory oaths is not itself proof that the practice falls outside Matthew's prohibition. Conversely, calling a statement an oath does not establish that Paul knowingly disregarded a historical instruction of Jesus.

## Competing readings

A broad reading of Matthew creates a substantive tension with invoking divine testimony. A narrower reading may distinguish formal swearing from rhetorical assurances. A third possibility is that Matthew preserves an ethical demand whose precise scope or transmission differs from Paul's circumstances. None establishes Paul's private motives.

James 5:12 supplies a relevant non-Pauline comparison for eventual review. Matthew 23:16–22 is relevant to oath distinctions. Neither is added as a Pauline witness.
