# Self-examination and the judging interlocutor

Luke 6:37–42 and 18:9–14 criticize judgment without self-awareness. Romans 2:1–5 similarly turns accusation back upon the accuser. Retain draft agreement.

## Checks
- Luke's image of impaired sight distinguishes self-correction from a blanket prohibition on correction.
- Romans 2 targets a judge who practices the conduct condemned; this does not demonstrate that all ethical assessment is forbidden.
- Scott Storbakken (2024) describes competing identifications of the interlocutor in Romans 2 and the dangers of historically anti-Jewish generalizations. The rhetorical figure must not be equated with Judaism as a whole.
- The Yonder Man and the Hypocrite (2023) interprets Romans 2:1–5 as exposing misplaced confidence in escaping judgment despite one's own wrongdoing.
- Second Corinthians 13:5 addresses examination in a dispute over apostolic authority; its purpose should not be collapsed into Luke's parable.
