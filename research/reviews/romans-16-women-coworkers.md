# Romans 16 — Phoebe, Prisca, Junia and women coworkers

**Issue:** #5 · **Status:** draft; claim-level source verification outstanding

## Textual inventory

| Reference | Woman / women | Explicit description | Limits of inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Romans 16:15 | Julia and Nereus's sister | Named among those greeted. | Greeting is evidence of inclusion, not a specified ministry role. |

## Interpretive tests

**Strong-participation reading:** The quantity and positive descriptions of women in Paul's personal network, especially Phoebe and Prisca, challenge claims that Paul uniformly excluded women from influential ministry.

**Office-distinction reading:** The letters distinguish coworkers, service, hosting, prophecy and oversight; it is not self-evident that these roles are interchangeable with later pastoral offices.

**Junia test:** The disputed prepositional phrase must be analyzed separately from the personal name and the question of what “apostle” signifies in this context. A woman named Junia is not itself sufficient to prove either disputed grammatical construal.

## Crosschecks

- 1 Corinthians 11:5 presumes women pray or prophesy, while 1 Corinthians 14:34–35 raises a separate speech restriction and textual-history dispute.
- Philippians 4:2–3 names Euodia and Syntyche as women who labored with Paul for the gospel.
- Acts 18:24–26 depicts Priscilla and Aquila instructing Apollos, but Acts is a separate narrative witness, not Paul's autobiography.
- 1 Timothy 2:11–15 belongs to a letter of disputed Pauline authorship; keep the historical and canonical questions distinct.

The sources disagree about the force of the phrase in Romans 16:7. Sybrandi's interpretation of translators' motivations is an argument made by that author, not an independently established fact about individual translators. No status promotion is warranted on this asymmetric source access.

**Disposition:** direct evidence of substantial female participation; exact offices and Junia's apostolic description remain contested.
