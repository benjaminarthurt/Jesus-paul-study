# Humility and status reversal in Philippians 2

## Assessment

Retain agreement provisionally. Luke 14:7–11 presents reversal of honor in a meal setting; Luke 9:46–48 redirects competition for greatness toward receiving a child. Philippians 2:1–11 addresses rivalry through the example of Christ. Romans 12 and 1 Corinthians 1 offer complementary communal arguments.

## Scholarly checks

Martin and Nash argue that the passage employs ancient hymnic conventions while overturning conventional honor through praise of lowly service. Tsui reads the passage as both ethical and soteriological, placing self-emptying within divine self-giving and renewed humanity. Fewster identifies unresolved questions concerning the origin, form and function of the composition. Thus ethical continuity does not establish literary dependence, common authorship or agreement on every Christological question.

## Limitations

Distinguish voluntary relinquishment of privilege from imposed humiliation. Neither passage settles institutional hierarchy or requires tolerating abuse. Full articles, independent Lukan scholarship, Greek collation and the relationship between humility and exaltation remain to be examined. Draft only.
