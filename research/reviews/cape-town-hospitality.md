# How the Cape Town Commitment reads hospitality

The Lausanne Movement addresses two audiences in part IIC.5. Christians in receiving communities are asked to open homes and offer practical help to newcomers, including students of other faiths. Christians who have migrated are addressed as people capable of shaping their new communities. Churches with different migration histories are asked to learn from each other. A congregation that treats all newcomers as prospective recipients misses one of the statement's two instructions. [Cape Town Commitment, IIC.5–6 and notes 75–78](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment)

The biblical references change with the audience. Note 76 joins Matthew's judgment scene and Luke's Samaritan with Romans 12:13 and several other texts to support the host's duties. Note 77 appeals to Jeremiah 29:7 for the migrant's contribution to the host community. This is direct evidence of how the statement uses scripture. Galatians 2 offers a further comparison about withdrawal from a table, although the statement does not cite that episode in these notes. [Matthew 25:34–40](/passages/mt-25-31-46/#unit-34-40-teaching) · [Comparison of table boundaries](/comparisons/outsiders-and-community-boundaries/)

Section IIC.6 places the freedom of other religious communities within the scope of Christian advocacy while retaining an explicit hope for conversion. That pairing matters when a Christian institution has power over someone who needs its help. Readers can ask whether a person is free to decline its invitation and still receive the same service. This practical test is an application of the commitment. The inspected English sections give no evidence about compliance by individual churches, reception among migrants or the effectiveness of any government policy.
