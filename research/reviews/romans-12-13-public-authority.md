# Romans 12–13: enemy love and public authority

Draft source audit. Publisher abstracts inspected; full articles and Greek apparatus not yet checked.

## Sources and competing readings

- Dorothea H. Bertschmann, [The Good, the Bad and the State – Rom 13.1–7 and the Dynamics of Love](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688513000350), New Testament Studies 60 (2014), 232–249. Abstract: distinguishes the believers' ethic of overcoming evil with good from the authorities' punitive justice; proposes compatibility rather than contradiction.
- David G. Horrell, [The Peaceable, Tolerant Community and the Legitimate Role of the State](https://doi.org/10.1177/003463730310000106), Review & Expositor 100 (2003), 81–99. Abstract: emphasizes the nonviolent community alongside the state's authorized force, leaving difficult questions about overlapping citizenship.
- David VanDrunen, [Power to the People: Revisiting Civil Resistance in Romans 13:1–7 in Light of the Noahic Covenant](https://doi.org/10.1017/jlr.2015.41), 2016. Abstract opening inspected: identifies the problem of unjust rulers and the limitations of what Romans explicitly addresses. The proposed covenant argument has not yet been examined.
