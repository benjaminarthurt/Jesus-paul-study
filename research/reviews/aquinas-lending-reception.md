# Aquinas: lending and economic justice

Draft reception study. [Summa Theologiae II-II, q. 78, a. 1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3078.htm), English translation inspected.

Aquinas treats charging for the use of money lent as unjust under his analysis of consumable goods. He interprets Luke 19:23 figuratively as spiritual increase rather than authorizing interest. His response to Luke 6:35 distinguishes the duty to lend in particular circumstances from the prohibition on profiting through a loan, and offers a further reading concerning trust in God. He distinguishes civil permission from moral approval.

This is primarily a Gospel reception and natural-law study; no explicit Pauline dependence is established by the inspected article. Compare the work-and-material-support and wealth studies as thematic connections only. His treatment of Jewish lending includes prejudicial generalization that should be studied critically, not repeated as historical fact. Modern loan structures and monetary institutions require separate economic and historical analysis.
