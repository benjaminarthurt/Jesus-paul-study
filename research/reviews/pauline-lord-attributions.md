# When Paul invokes the Lord

Who supplies the words, who authorizes them, and who applies them? A reader who searches only for “Lord” will mix several kinds of evidence. Six chapters inspected in WEBP illustrate the differences. Each linked record is a draft paraphrase with its primary chapter attached.

| Passage | What the writer attributes | Where the attribution stops or becomes uncertain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1 Corinthians 7:10–16](/passages/1co-07-10-16/) | In 10 the writer introduces a marital instruction with the Lord as its authority. | The parenthesis in 11 addresses a separation that has already happened. Its exact relation to the inherited instruction is unresolved. Verse 12 explicitly introduces Paul's own direction to a couple in a different situation. |
| [1 Corinthians 9:14–18](/passages/1co-09-01-18/) | Verse 14 reports a ruling that gospel workers may receive their living from their work. | Paul explains his own decision to forgo support. Waiving a right is compatible with acknowledging that others possess it. The letter gives no setting or transcript of Jesus delivering this ruling. |
| [1 Corinthians 11:23–26](/passages/1co-11-17-34/) | Verse 23 introduces a received account. The cited speaker over the bread and cup in 24–25 is Jesus. | In 26 the writer explains what the eaters proclaim. An immediate personal revelation and transmission through a community are different accounts of how Paul received the tradition. The formula alone cannot choose between them. |
| [1 Corinthians 14:37–38](/pauline-teachings/#1co-t-14-37-38) | The writer asks anyone claiming prophetic or spiritual standing to recognize his written directions as commanded by the Lord. | This is a claim about the authority of the letter. It supplies no earlier words of Jesus. Its scope also matters to the disputed place and voice of 34–35. |
| [1 Thessalonians 4:13–18](/passages/1th-04-13-18/) | In 15 the writers invoke a word from the Lord in assuring mourners that the living have no priority over the dead. | Verses 16–17 develop the assurance. The letter gives no explicit closing boundary for the attributed word. Verse 18 addresses the community with an instruction to console. |
| [2 Corinthians 12:7–10](/pauline-teachings/#2co-t-12-09-09) | Paul reports a reply to his repeated prayer. The response occupies the opening part of verse 9. | The decision to boast about weakness in the second part of the verse belongs to Paul. The narrated occasion is his own prayer, distinct from an episode in Jesus' earthly ministry. |

## A Greek distinction that changes the comparison

In 1 Corinthians 7:10 the first-person verb **παραγγέλλω** introduces the charge, but the following contrast credits the Lord. Verse 25 uses **γνώμην** for the judgment Paul offers in a case where he says he has no command from the Lord. The same noun returns in 40 alongside Paul's claim to possess God's Spirit. Personal judgment therefore has a recognizable status within the letter even where Paul gives it theological weight. At 7:5 WEBP mentions fasting alongside prayer, while the displayed SBLGNT main text has prayer alone. That difference limits what can be claimed about the purpose of temporary abstinence across editions. The readings were compared on the two edition pages. Manuscript witnesses and their dates were not independently collated. [Greek edition](/sources/#source-sblgnt-1co7) · [English chapter](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1CO07.htm)

## Why inventories differ

Ellis defines a tradition narrowly enough to require evidence of prior use. An explicit attribution inside a letter answers a question about its argument while leaving the history of transmission open. Becker distinguishes citation of a saying, a claim to Jesus' authority and a possible analogy to Gospel material. Hiestermann includes 1 Corinthians 14:37 and 1 Thessalonians 4:15–17 when delimiting a broader set of explicit references. These approaches can count different things even when their authors read the same verses. The catalog therefore labels the claim in 14:37 separately and flags the possible wider boundary in 1 Thessalonians.
