# Corinthian community boundaries and judgment

## Question

Does Paul's disciplinary separation in 1 Corinthians 5 conflict with the neighbor ethic in Luke 10:25–37 or the tolerance of an independent exorcist in Luke 9:49–50? Retain different emphasis provisionally.

## Claim checks

1. First Corinthians 5:9–13 distinguishes dealings with outsiders from discipline within the assembly. The restriction is not a universal demand to avoid nonbelievers.
2. Bosworth interprets expulsion in terms of group identity and public reputation: the particular offense undermines the community's ethical distinctiveness. This does not make every form of exclusion equivalent.
3. Moses argues the punishment carries a spiritual dimension beyond removal from the gathering, with an ultimate restorative purpose. This is an interpretation, not an uncontested account of the mechanism.
4. McDonough identifies a possible structural dependence on Deuteronomy 17 across 1 Corinthians 5–6. The quotation in 5:13 warrants attention alongside Paul's ensuing discussion of judicial competence.
5. Luke's neighbor illustration addresses assistance and mercy rather than membership procedures. The independent exorcist episode concerns a different kind of boundary. Neither establishes the absence of disciplinary rules in the Gospel traditions.
6. Collins cautions that the Corinthian correspondence is occasion-specific and preserves only part of the conversation. Reconstructions of Paul's motives require restraint.

## Competing readings

Compassion and internal discipline may coexist. A stronger ethical tension emerges if exclusion prevents provision of basic care or becomes an instrument of coercive control. Neither modern application nor hypothetical abuse establishes a contradiction in the original texts. Matthew 18:15–17 and 2 Corinthians 2:5–11 require separate comparison; the identity of the disciplined individual in the latter passage must not be assumed.
