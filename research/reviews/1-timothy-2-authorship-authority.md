# 1 Timothy 2:8–15 — authorship, teaching and authority

**Issue:** #5 · **Status:** preliminary draft

## Separate questions

1. **Text:** What does 1 Timothy 2:11–15 direct its audience to do?
2. **Semantics:** What is the force of `authentein` in verse 12 and its relation to teaching?
3. **Context:** Is the prohibition local, office-specific, or universal?
4. **Authorship:** Is the letter historically written by Paul? Scholarly opinion is divided, with many critical scholars regarding the Pastorals as non-Pauline and others defending Pauline authorship.
5. **Reception:** How should a contemporary church weigh a canonical letter even if its historical authorship is disputed?

These are not interchangeable questions. An argument for disputed authorship is not a claim that the text is absent from the canon; an argument for canonical authority does not independently establish historical Pauline authorship.

| 1 Timothy 2:13–14 | An appeal is made to Adam and Eve. | How creation-order reasoning relates to local circumstances and normative scope. |
| 1 Timothy 2:15 | A difficult statement connects salvation and childbearing with faith/love/holiness. | Referent, grammar and interpretation remain contested. |
| 1 Timothy 3:1–13 | Overseer and deacon qualifications follow. | Whether 2:12 specifically concerns the office described in chapter 3. |

## Competing readings

**Continuing office restriction:** Creation appeal and surrounding overseer material indicate a lasting restriction on authoritative teaching/oversight; proponents must reconcile this with female coworkers and prophecy in undisputed letters.

**Local corrective restriction:** False teaching or abusive behavior in the local community may explain the prohibition; proponents must show evidence for the proposed situation and explain the creation appeal.

**Historical-development reading:** Differences from undisputed Paul may reflect later church ordering; this requires independent authorship arguments, not merely labeling an inconvenient verse inauthentic.
