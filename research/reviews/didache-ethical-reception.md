# Didache: ethical instruction and community order

Draft historical reception study. Primary source: [Didache, chapters 1–6](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0714.htm), English translation excerpts inspected.

## Observations

- Chapter 1 combines neighbor love, enemy love, nonretaliation and generosity in a practical ethical curriculum. Its qualifications concerning recipients invite comparison with later communal support policies.
- Chapters 2–3 connect truthful speech, anger and inward dispositions to concrete conduct.
- Chapter 4 joins material sharing to communal correction and describes obligations within slaveholding households. Its recognition of shared religious standing does not abolish slavery.
- Chapter 6 permits differentiated capacity to bear religious obligations but strongly cautions against idol food. Compare Romans 14 and 1 Corinthians 8–10 without assuming identical situations.

## Research implications

Compare Matthew 5, Luke 6, Romans 12 and the household instructions of Ephesians and Colossians. These are thematic parallels, not established literary dependence. The latter letters have disputed authorship. The coexistence of radical generosity and inherited social hierarchy deserves investigation rather than selective quotation.

## Limitations

Date, compositional layers, Greek wording, literary dependencies and independent scholarship remain unverified. Later chapters require separate review.
