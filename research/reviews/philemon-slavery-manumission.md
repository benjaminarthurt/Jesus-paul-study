## Claim-level verification

- Philemon 15–16: relational brotherhood is explicit in the letter, but a legally enforceable manumission request is disputed. Arinder and de Vos offer contrasting readings; Uzukwu challenges the assumption that relational change is emancipatory.
- Philemon 8–21: Paul's appeal and expectation of compliance require examination as exercises of social authority; Onesimus's own account is absent. Tolmie's reception study provides a route for examining interpretive silences.
- Roman emancipation: legal release and actual social independence were not necessarily equivalent. Nasrallah and Green identify relevant historical complications; inspect primary legal evidence before making precise claims.
- General abolition: a proposed individual emancipation does not establish an abolitionist program. Conversely, absence of explicit abolition is not sufficient evidence for every possible claim about Paul's moral endorsement.
- Gospel comparison: Mark 10:35–45 reverses conventional status hierarchy, but does not expressly legislate abolition. Compare its service imagery with Philemon and Galatians 3:28 without collapsing metaphor, legal status and ethics.
- Authorship: distinguish Ephesians and Colossians household codes from undisputed Pauline letters.
