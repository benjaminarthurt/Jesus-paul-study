# Final judgment in an Orthodox Church in America catechetical text

The Orthodox Church in America's online *The Orthodox Faith*, volume IV, presents final judgment as an account of words and actions before Christ. It cites Matthew on speech and recompense, John 5 on the Son's authority to judge, and Matthew 25 on care for people who are hungry, sick, strangers or imprisoned. The cited passages have distinct functions. John identifies the judge; Matthew supplies the scene through which the chapter explains service. [Primary catechetical text, “The Final Judgment”](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-kingdom-of-heaven/the-final-judgment)

The chapter interprets the least of Christ's brethren broadly. It argues that serving someone in distress reaches Christ himself. It connects that identification with Christ's own hunger, thirst, vulnerability and suffering and invokes the image language of Colossians 1:15. This documents a contemporary use of a traditionally Pauline letter whose authorship is disputed. It does not establish that Matthew derived the judgment scene from Colossians. Nor does the chapter's broad application settle which people Matthew's first audience would have understood the phrase to designate. [Matthew 25:31–46](/passages/mt-25-31-46/) · [Colossians](/books/col/)

The chapter treats care as consequential at the judgment. Luther's chief article asks how justification can be received. Putting those answers side by side reveals a difference of question before it establishes any doctrinal conflict. Determining whether their accounts of grace and conduct are ultimately compatible would require sources in which each tradition addresses both questions. [Luther's chief article](/research-reviews/luther-chief-article/) · [Salvation and judgment](/comparisons/salvation-and-judgment/)

The English chapter was inspected on November 27, 2026. Its references to Augustine and Simeon are evidence for the catechist's use of tradition. Their original texts were not inspected for this study. One institution's catechetical publication cannot establish the distribution of interpretations across Orthodox jurisdictions.
