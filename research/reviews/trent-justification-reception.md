# Trent: justification, grace and works (1547)

Draft historical reception study. [Session VI, Decree on Justification](https://adfonteslibrary.org/work-council-trent-session-06), chapters VII–XI and canons I, IV, IX–XI, selected English text inspected.

Chapter VII brings James 2, Galatians 5:6 and Matthew 19:17 into a common account of faith and obedience. Chapter VIII denies that either faith or works prior to justification earn its grace. Chapter X describes growth in received righteousness through faith and action, and Chapter XI maintains the obligation to obey divine commandments. Canons I and IV reject both justification by unaided works and an entirely passive account of human cooperation; IX–XI reject specified understandings of faith alone and imputation alone.

Comparison with Augsburg IV shows shared denial of independently earned justification but a substantive difference over the relation of faith, grace, transformed conduct and justification. The documents use different theological definitions; this is not a simple binary of Jesus versus Paul. Trent directly incorporates both Gospel and Pauline material, as well as other New Testament witnesses.
