# Aquinas: nonretaliation and public war

Draft reception study. [Summa Theologiae II-II, q. 40, a. 1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3040.htm), English translation, article 1 inspected.

The objections explicitly invoke Matthew 26:52, Matthew 5:39 and Romans 12:19. Aquinas answers with three conditions for just war: public authority, just cause and right intention. He explicitly invokes Romans 13:4 to extend the public ruler's protective responsibility to external conflict. Drawing on Augustine, he interprets nonretaliation as a disposition that may coexist with action for the common good. This documents a medieval reconciliation of Gospel and Pauline passages, not proof their original authors intended the same distinction.

The reference to Luke 3:14 concerns John the Baptist, not Jesus. Distinguish the objections from Aquinas's own conclusions. Compare the project's enemy-love-and-civil-authority study and Augustine's coercion study. Do not treat medieval criteria as identical to modern law.
