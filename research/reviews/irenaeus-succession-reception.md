# Irenaeus: apostolic succession and doctrinal boundaries

Draft reception study. [Against Heresies III.3](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103303.htm), sections 1–4, English translation inspected.

Irenaeus appeals to public episcopal succession against claims of secret apostolic instruction. He associates Roman tradition with both Peter and Paul, cites Clement's letter to Corinth, and invokes Polycarp as a witness to apostolic continuity. Section 4 explicitly applies Titus 3:10 to rejection of doctrinal opponents. Titus belongs to the disputed Pauline corpus in this project.

Compare Gospel service leadership, Corinthian discipline and the Pastoral Epistles. The text demonstrates reception of Pauline-attributed authority, not that historical Paul established the later episcopal system. Irenaeus's historical claims and the scope of Roman authority require independent verification.
