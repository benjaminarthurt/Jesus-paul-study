# Romans 2: faith, deeds and final judgment

Draft review. Access recorded below is limited to retrieved extracts, abstracts and selected introductory passages.

## Scholarly evidence

- Nigel M. Watson, [Justified by Faith; judged by Works – an Antinomy?](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688500010729), NTS 29 (1983), 209–221. Opening extract identifies both themes across undisputed Pauline letters. Final resolution not inspected.
- Klyne R. Snodgrass, [Justification by Grace–to the Doers](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688500013515), NTS 32 (1986), 72–93. Opening extract urges attention to the real significance of judgment by works, discussing Donfried's account of final judgment for Christians. Do not equate Donfried's precise argument with Snodgrass's uninspected conclusion.
- Philip La Grange Du Toit, [Paul's Radicalisation of Law-obedience in Romans 2](https://doi.org/10.4102/ids.v50i1.2034), In die Skriflig 50 (2016). Abstract and introduction inspected: interprets law-obedience as a condition belonging to the pre-Christ era and faith as the new condition. Lists five interpretive approaches, including unattainable obedience and Christian fulfillment.
- Kevin W. McFadden, [Judgment According to Works in Romans](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctt22nm8n0), Fortress Press (2013). Book description and chapter previews: understands accusation and ethical exhortation as different functions of judgment and argues for coherence between faith and final judgment.
- S. J. Gathercole, [A Law unto Themselves](https://doi.org/10.1177/0142064X0202400302), JSNT 24.3 (2002). Abstract: identifies the Gentiles in Romans 2:14–15 with fulfillment of new-covenant promises rather than merely natural-law awareness.

## Claim matrix

| Question | Primary evidence | Competing readings and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is judgment by deeds exclusive to Jesus? | Matthew 25:31–46; Romans 2:6–16; 2 Corinthians 5:10 | No: Paul uses deed-based judgment language. Watson identifies the tension within Paul himself. The theological function remains disputed. |
| Are the doers of Torah actually justified? | Romans 2:13 contrasted with 3:20–31 | Du Toit locates the requirement in the old era; Gathercole connects obedience with new-covenant Gentiles. Neither abstract settles every clause. |
| Is Paul contradicting himself? | Romans 2–3; Galatians 2–5 | McFadden argues for complementary functions; a divergence reading must explain why both themes recur. Distinguish coherence proposed by a scholar from demonstrated coherence. |
| What role does obedience have in final salvation? | Romans 2:6–11; Galatians 5:6; 2 Corinthians 5:10 | Snodgrass's extract raises the significance of obedient Christian life. Distinguish evidence of faith, condition, reward and basis of justification. |
| Is Matthew 25 a universal humanitarian judgment? | Matthew 25:31–46 | Identity of the least brothers remains disputed; this batch has not examined Matthew specialists. Do not assume that the passage directly formulates Paul's justification categories. |
