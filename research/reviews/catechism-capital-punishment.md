# The 2018 Catholic Catechism revision on capital punishment

The revised paragraph 2267 is unusually informative about change over time. It begins by describing an earlier understanding in which a legitimate authority, after a fair trial, could regard execution as an extreme means of safeguarding the community. It then gives three reasons for a changed conclusion: the offender retains personal dignity, the purpose of punishment is understood differently, and detention can protect others while preserving the offender's possibility of redemption. The revised judgment calls for worldwide abolition. [Official English text of the revision](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/ladaria-ferrer/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20180801_catechismo-penadimorte_en.html)

The paragraph invokes the Gospel and cites an address by Francis. It does not give a verse-by-verse interpretation of Romans 13 or establish that Paul had this policy in view. Its appeal to the capacity of detention is especially important: part of its reasoning concerns what institutions can do in the circumstances it describes. A reader who accepts a public duty to protect others can still dispute which penalties are necessary or permissible.
