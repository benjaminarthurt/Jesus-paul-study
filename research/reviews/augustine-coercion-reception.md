# Augustine: religious coercion and corrective love

Draft reception study. [Letter 93 to Vincentius (408)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1102093.htm), chapters 1–2, sections 1–4, English translation inspected.

Augustine defends legal pressure against Donatists as a means of correction. He argues that restraint can benefit the person restrained and combines fear of penalties with instruction. He reinterprets nonretaliation as compatible with coercive intervention intended to restore religious unity, while distinguishing mere intimidation from instruction.

Compare Romans 12:17–21, Romans 13:1–7 and Matthew 5:38–48. The nonretaliation connection is thematic; explicit Pauline attribution and dependence on Romans 13 require further examination. His reports of beneficial outcomes are his own claims, not independent evidence of effectiveness or consent.
