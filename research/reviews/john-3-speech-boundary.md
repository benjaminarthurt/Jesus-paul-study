# John 3:10–21 — speech boundary review

**Issue:** #2 · **Status:** draft, preliminary passage-level analysis · **Reviewed status:** not claimed

## Question

At what point does Jesus' dialogue with Nicodemus end and the evangelist's explanatory narration begin? Ancient Greek manuscripts did not supply modern quotation marks, so the familiar red-letter boundary is an editorial judgment rather than a punctuation datum in the earliest text.

## Passage map

| Verses | Attribution confidence | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3:1–2 | Narrator / Nicodemus | Narrative introduction and explicitly introduced Nicodemus speech |
| 3:3–10 | Jesus and Nicodemus | Repeated explicit dialogue markers |
| 3:11–12 | Jesus, strongly indicated | Continuation of Jesus' direct second-person reply |
| 3:13–15 | Often treated as Jesus' continued discourse | No explicit new narrator tag; the shift toward third-person Son-of-Man exposition creates debate |
| 3:16–21 | Disputed: Jesus' speech or evangelist's exposition | Generalizing language and theological vocabulary can be read as a Johannine editorial explanation; there is no unambiguous new speech tag |

**Important:** This map describes interpretive options, not a critical-edition verdict. The absence of quotation marks in early witnesses does not prove either endpoint.

## Two plausible editorial conventions

1. **Extended-speech convention:** Jesus' words run through 3:21, reading 3:16–21 as the theological climax of his conversation. This preserves the uninterrupted flow of the discourse.
2. **Narrator-transition convention:** Jesus' direct speech ends around 3:15, with 3:16–21 explaining the significance of the dialogue in the evangelist's voice. This fits the Gospel's recurrent interpretive narrator and the passage's thematic generalizations.

Some interpreters place the transition elsewhere within 3:13–21; the choice is not binary. Modern translation quotation marks, paragraphing, and red-letter printing should be compared explicitly before choosing a display boundary.

## Implications for the project

- Do **not** unconditionally label John 3:16 as a directly spoken saying of the historical Jesus.
- Distinguish **words attributed to Jesus by a translation's editorial convention** from **the theological teaching of the Fourth Gospel**.
- For Jesus–Paul comparisons, do not silently treat Johannine narrative exposition as direct Jesus speech.
- Keep all affected records in `draft` pending source and editorial verification.

## Competing readings to preserve

**Extended-speech reading:** The lack of an explicit transition supports continuous Jesus speech; shifts in register are compatible with a sustained discourse.

**Narrator-transition reading:** Johannine explanatory style and thematic framing make 3:16–21 a natural evangelist comment; punctuation is a later editorial decision.

Neither interpretation establishes the exact historical words spoken in a private conversation.
