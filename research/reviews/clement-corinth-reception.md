# First Clement: humility, authority and Pauline reception

Traditionally dated to the late first century, this letter from the Roman church to Corinth addresses communal division. Dating and attribution require further scholarly verification.

## Primary evidence

[First Clement](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1010.htm), chapters 1–3, 5, 7–10, 12 (English translation excerpts retrieved). Chapters 1–3 associate peace with humility, hospitality and respect for presbyters. Chapter 5 presents Paul and Peter as examples of endurance. Chapters 7–10 connect repentance, divine mercy, scriptural examples and obedience. Chapter 12 presents Rahab as an example of faith and hospitality.

## Comparative significance

The letter combines moral instruction with institutional concerns. Compare Luke 9:46–48 and Philippians 2:1–11 on humility, and 1 Corinthians 1 on division. These are proposed research comparisons, not established literary dependencies. Paul's explicit appearance in chapter 5 establishes reception of his reputation, not dependence on every Pauline teaching.
