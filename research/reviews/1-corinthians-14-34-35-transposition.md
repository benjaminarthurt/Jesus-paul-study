# 1 Corinthians 14:34–35 — transposition, attribution and women's speech

**Issue:** #5 · **Status:** draft, not independently critical-apparatus verified

## Why this passage requires special handling

In the familiar order of 1 Corinthians 14, verses 34–35 instruct women to be silent in the assemblies and ask questions at home. Some Western textual witnesses instead place these verses after verse 40. This is a **transposition** (location difference), which is distinct from evidence that the verses were entirely absent. A location variant can be invoked in arguments for interpolation, but does not itself prove interpolation.

## Evidence and inference matrix

| Observation | What it supports | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Corinthians 11:5 describes women praying and prophesying | Some female speech is presupposed within the letter's discussion of worship. | That every kind of speech was unrestricted. |
| 1 Corinthians 14:26–33 regulates prophecy, tongues and orderly participation | Chapter 14 is concerned with how speech is managed in gatherings. | That 14:34–35 necessarily concerns only one particular type of disruption. |
| 14:34–35 in the common position after verse 33 | The transmitted text contains a restrictive instruction. | That the instruction is necessarily a universal rule for every era and setting. |
| Western witnesses placing 14:34–35 after verse 40 | The passage has a meaningful location variant in transmission. | That a complete omission is documented by those same witnesses, or that a later insertion is proven. |
| 14:36 contains a rhetorical challenge | A quotation-and-refutation interpretation is possible. | That the quotation begins and ends at the proposed points without further linguistic argument. |
| Romans 16:1–16 and Philippians 4:2–3 | Paul's letters name women as coworkers and participants in ministry. | That all these women held identical institutional offices. |

## Interpretive alternatives

**Authentic, context-limited regulation:** Paul may be restricting questions, interruptions or assessment of prophetic speech while permitting other forms of prayer and prophecy. The restriction's exact scope remains disputed.

**Quotation/refutation:** The restrictive language could reproduce a Corinthian position that Paul challenges in verse 36. The proposal must account for discourse markers and the absence of modern quotation punctuation.

**Interpolation:** The verses could be a later addition, with relocation reflecting textual instability. This requires both external manuscript and internal literary evidence; transposition alone is insufficient.

**Authentic, broader restriction:** Some interpret the verses as a general restriction on women's assembly speech, requiring an explanation for 11:5 and the letter's broader evidence of women participating.

## Methodological guardrails

- Separate **transposition**, **omission**, **authorship**, **quotation attribution**, and **normative application** as different claims.
- Distinguish 1 Timothy 2 (disputed Pauline authorship) from undisputed 1 Corinthians; canonical comparison is valid, but historical attribution cannot be assumed.
- Do not claim a critical Greek apparatus has been checked merely because translation notes or scholarly abstracts mention a Western location variant.
- Do not infer that women were entirely absent from leadership merely from one passage, or that modern institutional equality follows automatically from Romans 16.
