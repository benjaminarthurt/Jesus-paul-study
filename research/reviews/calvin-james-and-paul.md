# Calvin reads James and Paul on Abraham

In *Institutes of the Christian Religion* III.17, Calvin states the apparent collision himself. Paul uses Abraham to defend justification by faith apart from works. James appeals to the offering of Isaac and speaks of justification by works. Calvin begins with a theological premise about the Holy Spirit's consistency, then argues that the passages address different errors and use the pivotal words differently. [Primary text, III.17, especially sections 11–12](https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes/institutes.v.xviii.html)

His argument has two parts. First, an assent that leaves a person unchanged is different from the trust that unites the believer to Christ. Second, being accounted righteous and showing that one is righteous perform different argumentative functions. On Calvin's reading, the later offering of Isaac manifests a righteousness already reckoned to Abraham when he believed. The chronology matters to his reasoning. Replacing it with a general observation that faith and conduct belong together would miss the specific argument. [Romans 4:1–12](/passages/ro-04-01-12/)

This is explicit reception of Paul and James. Neither speaker in that exchange is Jesus. A further inference would be required to apply Calvin's distinction to the king's verdicts in Matthew 25, where the narrative identifies concrete acts toward others. Whether James intends exactly the demonstrative sense Calvin proposes also remains a question for exegesis. His premise about inspiration supplies a reason for him to seek reconciliation, separate from the evidence that his reading fits either passage.
