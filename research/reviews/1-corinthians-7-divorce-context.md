# 1 Corinthians 7: divorce, separation and remarriage

Draft source audit; selected article excerpts and publisher abstracts inspected, not complete critical-edition verification.

## Sources

- David Instone-Brewer, [1 Corinthians 7 in the Light of the Graeco-Roman Marriage and Divorce Papyri](https://doi.org/10.53751/001C.30262), Tyndale Bulletin 52.1 (2001), 101–116. Summary inspected: legal papyri illuminate divorce through separation and the chapter's lack of explicit remarriage discussion.
- David Instone-Brewer, [1 Corinthians 7 in the Light of the Jewish Greek and Aramaic Marriage and Divorce Papyri](https://instonebrewer.com/publications/TynBull_2001_52_2_04_Brewer_1Cor7DivorcePapyri.pdf), Tyndale Bulletin 52.2 (2001), 225–243. Page 243 conclusion excerpt inspected: argues that involuntary divorce can release the party from marital obligations, including allowing remarriage. This is the author's reconstruction, not Paul's express wording.
- Jerome Murphy-O'Connor, [The Divorced Woman in 1 Corinthians 7:10–11](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199564156.003.0004), in Keys to First Corinthians (2009), 32–42. Publisher abstract inspected: proposes a specific Corinthian case behind verses 10–11 and distinguishes it from the permission in verse 15.
