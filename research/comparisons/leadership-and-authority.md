## Textual comparison
Mark 9 and 10 associate greatness with service and reject the domination practiced by rulers. Matthew 23 criticizes status-seeking teachers and relativizes titles among disciples. Luke 12 warns servants entrusted with responsibility against abusing others.

Philippians 2 presents Christ's humility as a model for community conduct. Philemon 8–21 contrasts Paul's ability to command with his decision to appeal. Yet 1 Corinthians 5 gives forceful instructions for discipline, and 1 Thessalonians 5 asks believers to respect those who labor and lead.

## Competing interpretations
Authority exercised as service can be consistent with Jesus' teaching; the presence of leadership does not establish domination. Conversely, an appeal to apostolic authority can become coercive, and the rhetoric of voluntary consent in Philemon deserves scrutiny given Paul's influence. Matthew's warnings about titles must be examined in their literary setting before treating them as a universal ban on institutional roles.

## Modern application
Clergy accountability, institutional hierarchy, spiritual abuse and leadership titles are separate questions. A claim to serve does not by itself establish accountable practice.
