## Research question

Does Jesus make salvation depend on conduct while Paul makes it depend on faith? The comparison must distinguish justification, entry into God's kingdom, final judgment, and the relationship between faith and action rather than treating these as interchangeable technical terms.

## Gospel evidence

[Matthew 25:31–46](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT25.htm) depicts the Son of Man judging people in relation to their treatment of the hungry, stranger, sick and imprisoned. The identity of the least ones in the scene is debated; the passage should not be reduced to a generic formula of salvation by charitable works.

[Matthew 19](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT19.htm) and [Mark 10](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MRK10.htm) connect a question about eternal life with commandments, relinquishing possessions and following Jesus. Both accounts also emphasize divine possibility where human possibility fails.

[Luke 13](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LUK13.htm) warns against presuming admission to the kingdom. [Luke 19](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LUK19.htm) associates Zacchaeus's encounter with restitution, generosity and an announcement of salvation. The narrative does not explicitly establish a universal causal sequence between payment and salvation.

[John 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JHN05.htm) combines belief and passage from death to life with resurrection and judgment involving deeds. John 3:16–21 is additional theological context, not an uncontested quotation of Jesus; see the [speech-boundary review](/research-reviews/john-3-speech-boundary/).

## Pauline evidence

[Galatians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL02.htm) and [Romans 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/ROM03.htm) contrast justification with works of the Law. The meaning and scope of works of the Law, and the relation between faith in Christ and Christ's faithfulness, require further linguistic and historical research.

However, [Romans 2:6–16](https://ebible.org/engwebp/ROM02.htm) describes impartial judgment according to deeds, and [2 Corinthians 5:1–10](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2CO05.htm) describes accountability before Christ's judgment seat. [Galatians 5:6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL05.htm) connects faith with love in action. These are essential counterexamples to the claim that Paul considers conduct irrelevant.

## Competing readings

- **Different questions:** Justification and final judgment may address distinct dimensions of salvation. This reading must explain how the dimensions relate rather than merely assigning them different labels.
- **Faith expressed in action:** Deeds may evidence genuine faith without functioning as an independent basis of justification. The selected texts do not uniformly state this causal model explicitly.
- **Substantive divergence:** A reading of Gospel judgment criteria as sufficient conditions and Pauline justification as excluding all human action may generate a conflict. Both premises need demonstration, particularly in light of Romans 2 and Galatians 5.
- **Rhetorical or hypothetical judgment:** Some interpretations treat Romans 2 as establishing a standard that exposes universal failure. Others understand its positive judgment language as describing a real possibility. Neither is settled here.

## Modern implications

Debates over religious affiliation, charitable conduct, conversion, and the salvation of non-Christians cannot be settled by isolating one verse. The texts differ in audience, genre, vocabulary and historical setting. No modern denominational doctrine is treated as the default interpretive framework.
