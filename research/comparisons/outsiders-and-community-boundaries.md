## Who counts as an outsider, and who gets to decide?

John reports trying to stop a man who invokes Jesus' name because he does not travel with their group. Jesus tells John to let him continue. The man already acts in Jesus' name. In the Samaritan story, by contrast, the wounded traveler's affiliations never enter the decision to help. Jesus ends by asking the lawyer to identify who acted as a neighbor. The answer points to what the Samaritan did for someone who could make no reciprocal commitment. [Luke 9:49–50](/teachings/lk-s-09-50-50/) · [Luke 10:25–37](/passages/lk-10-25-37/)

Paul draws two different table boundaries. He tells Corinth to withdraw from a person claiming membership while engaging in specified misconduct. His clarification presupposes continued contact with outsiders. At Antioch, Paul objects when Cephas withdraws from eating with Gentile believers under pressure. In Paul's telling, the withdrawal presses Gentiles to adopt Jewish practices. Membership protects the Gentile believers' place at this table while exposing a member at Corinth to discipline. The difference turns on the grounds for exclusion. [1 Corinthians 5:9–13](/pauline-teachings/1co-t-05-09-13/) · [Galatians 2:11–14](/pauline-teachings/ga-t-02-11-14/)

## Judgment and its limits

The strongest point of agreement is that standing outside a group supplies no sufficient reason to obstruct someone or sever ordinary contact. The sharpest tension is practical. Jesus makes costly assistance exemplary, while Paul's instruction can deprive someone of fellowship and a shared meal. A direct contradiction would require evidence that they prescribe incompatible responses to the same person in the same circumstances. Neither writer addresses whether the disciplined member should receive emergency help. **Different emphasis** remains the draft assessment. First Thessalonians adds a further audience distinction. Its work instruction addresses believers concerned with how outsiders see them. Turning it into a work test imposed on outsiders reverses the direction of the instruction. [1 Thessalonians 4:9–12](/pauline-teachings/1th-t-04-09-12/) · [Corinthian research review](/research-reviews/corinthian-community-boundaries/)

The accounts leave the perspective of the injured traveler, the excluded member and the Gentile diners largely unreported. Galatians gives Paul's side of the Antioch dispute. The social extent of the Corinthian meal restriction and its enforcement remain open to specialist review.
