## Gospel evidence

[Matthew 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT06.htm) connects forgiving others with divine forgiveness. [Matthew 18](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT18.htm) combines a process for addressing wrongdoing with repeated forgiveness and a parable condemning withheld mercy. The disciplinary process precedes the forgiveness teaching in the same chapter.

[Luke 17](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LUK17.htm) explicitly connects rebuke, repentance and repeated forgiveness. [Luke 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LUK06.htm) warns against condemnation and urges forgiveness. These passages do not reduce forgiveness to a single procedural rule.

## Pauline evidence

[1 Corinthians 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1CO05.htm) calls for exclusion in a specific case of misconduct, distinguishes insiders from outsiders and expresses a salvific purpose for the disciplinary action.

[2 Corinthians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2CO02.htm) urges forgiveness and comfort following punishment by the majority, lest the person be overwhelmed by sorrow. Paul calls for renewed affirmation of love. The identity of this offender with the person in 1 Corinthians 5 is disputed and is not assumed here.

[Romans 12](https://ebible.org/engwebp/ROM12.htm) rejects personal vengeance and calls for peace where possible.

## Competing readings

- **Complementary practices:** Forgiveness and accountability coexist in both corpora. Matthew 18 provides a direct counterexample to the claim that Jesus never permits communal exclusion.
- **Different emphasis:** Jesus foregrounds mercy and repeated forgiveness, while Paul's letters address particular community crises and disciplinary decisions. Genre and occasion matter.
- **Potential tension:** A punitive reading of 1 Corinthians 5 may conflict with an expansive reading of Jesus' mercy. Establishing contradiction requires showing that the same action is prohibited and required under comparable circumstances.

## Modern implications

Forgiveness does not necessarily entail immediate restoration of trust, removal of protective boundaries, or abandonment of legal accountability. Ancient assembly discipline cannot be transferred directly into modern criminal justice or safeguarding policies without additional ethical and historical argument.

## Disputed-authorship contextual witnesses

These references are provisional contextual evidence, not an attribution of their claims to the historical Paul.
