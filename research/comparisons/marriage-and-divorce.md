## Research question

The Gospel traditions restrict divorce and remarriage, while Paul distinguishes a command attributed to the Lord from his own guidance about marriages between believers and unbelievers. Does this distinction represent a contradiction, a contextual extension, or an application of an existing principle?

## Gospel evidence

[Mark 10:2–12](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MRK10.htm) presents Jesus appealing to creation and the union of spouses, rejecting divorce followed by remarriage as adultery. The formulation addresses both husbands and wives.

[Matthew 19:1–12](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT19.htm) shares the creation argument but includes an exception involving sexual immorality. Matthew 5:31–32 also contains an exception formulation. The scope of the Greek term *porneia* and whether the exception permits remarriage require separate investigation.

[Luke 16:18](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LUK16.htm) presents a concise prohibition without an explicit exception. These differences within the Gospel traditions must be acknowledged before constructing a single universal rule attributed to Jesus.

## Paul's distinction

[1 Corinthians 7:10–16](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1CO07.htm) first attributes a restriction on separation to the Lord, with reconciliation or remaining unmarried as options if separation occurs. Paul then distinguishes his own guidance concerning mixed-belief marriages. A believer should not dismiss a spouse willing to remain, but if the unbelieving spouse departs, Paul permits the departure and says the believer is not enslaved in such circumstances.

The phrase distinguishing Paul's instruction from the Lord's may indicate that Paul knows no transmitted teaching of Jesus directly addressing this situation. It does not by itself prove that Paul considers his guidance devoid of religious authority.

## Competing readings

- **Contextual extension:** Paul preserves the prohibition against initiating divorce while addressing abandonment, a circumstance not explicitly treated in the selected Gospel sayings.
- **Substantive exception:** Paul's release from bondage introduces a new exception to a more restrictive teaching. Whether that release includes permission to remarry is disputed.
- **Compatible separation without remarriage:** Paul permits the departing spouse to leave but does not explicitly authorize remarriage in verse 15. This reading must account for the meaning and practical implications of not being enslaved.

## Modern implications

Divorce, remarriage, religious differences, abandonment and domestic abuse are distinct issues. The selected passages do not explicitly resolve every modern case. In particular, a restriction on divorce cannot automatically be treated as an instruction to remain physically exposed to abuse. Immediate safety, legal protection and theological judgments about marital status must be distinguished.

**Disposition:** substantial continuity in the restriction on divorce, with apparent tension concerning abandonment and the scope of exceptions. Direct contradiction is not established.

These references are provisional contextual evidence, not an attribution of their claims to the historical Paul.
