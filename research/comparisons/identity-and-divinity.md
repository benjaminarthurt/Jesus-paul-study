## Textual comparison

Luke narrates Peter's messianic confession and Jesus' suffering prediction. John presents Jesus' relation to the Father, authority over life and Thomas's confession. Paul identifies Jesus as Lord in a creation-related formula and presents humiliation and exaltation in Philippians 2; 1 Corinthians 15 also describes the Son's ultimate subjection to God.

## Competing interpretations and limitations

Continuity in elevated status is plausible, but the precise relationship between divine identity, agency, preexistence and exaltation is debated. Thomas's confession is his speech, not Jesus' own self-description. Later creedal formulations must not be read back as uncontested meanings.

## Modern implications

Distinguish historical Christology from later doctrinal judgments and modern denominational disputes.
