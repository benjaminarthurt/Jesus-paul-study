## Textual comparison

Matthew 5 extends adultery language to intentional desire; Matthew 19 and Mark 10 address marital fidelity and divorce. Paul in 1 Corinthians 6 grounds sexual conduct in bodily union and belonging to Christ, while chapters 5 and 7 address specific community and marriage questions.

## Competing interpretations and limitations

Both corpora place demands on sexual conduct. Paul's community-specific rules extend beyond the selected Gospel sayings, but silence in one corpus does not establish contradiction. The meaning and scope of sexual immorality terminology require historical analysis.

## Modern implications

Modern questions of consent, exploitation, sexual identity and marital obligations require distinct analysis rather than treating every ancient term as a direct modern category.
