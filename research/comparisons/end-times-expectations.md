## Textual comparison

Mark 13 combines gathering imagery, a generation saying and uncertainty about the day or hour. Matthew 24 similarly urges watchfulness. Paul speaks of the dead rising and the living meeting the Lord, using first-person plural language, while 1 Thessalonians 5 emphasizes unexpected timing.

## Competing interpretations and limitations

Imminent expectation is a serious reading of both corpora. Whether the generation saying refers to temple events, final consummation or another referent remains disputed. Paul's inclusive language need not be a categorical prediction of his own survival. Later theological harmonizations require independent argument.

## Modern implications

Avoid date-setting and treating modern events as proven fulfillments of ancient imagery.

## Disputed-authorship contextual witnesses

Second Thessalonians introduces preceding events and warns against claims that the day has arrived. Whether this qualifies, develops or reflects a different author from First Thessalonians requires separate historical analysis. These references are provisional contextual evidence, not an attribution of their claims to the historical Paul.
