## Textual comparison

John 14 presents Jesus as the way to the Father. Luke 9 discourages stopping an outsider acting in Jesus' name, and Mark 2 depicts association with people labeled sinners. Matthew 28 commissions disciples among all nations. Paul argues for Gentile inclusion without requiring circumcision and describes unity in Christ.

## Competing interpretations and limitations

Inclusion across ethnic and social boundaries is not identical to religious pluralism. Luke's outsider acts in Jesus' name, so the episode cannot alone establish universal salvation. John's claims and Paul's Christ-centered inclusion require separate attention to their respective audiences.

## Modern implications

Distinguish civil religious freedom, interfaith cooperation, conversion and theological claims about salvation.
