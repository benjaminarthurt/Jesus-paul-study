## Textual comparison
Matthew 6 criticizes prayer performed to gain public recognition and offers a prayer model. Luke 11 encourages asking and Luke 18 uses a parable to encourage persistence. Mark 11 associates prayer with faith and forgiveness; verse 26 has a textual variant and is not necessary to the comparison.

Philippians 4 encourages prayer and thanksgiving in the face of anxiety. First Thessalonians 5 calls for continuing prayer. First Corinthians 11 presupposes women and men praying and prophesying while regulating their conduct.

## Competing interpretations
Jesus' criticism targets motivation rather than every instance of communal prayer. Paul's assembly instructions address practice in a community and need not contradict private prayer. A stronger reading of Matthew's instruction to pray privately would require explanation of collective prayer elsewhere in the Gospel tradition and the distinction between public performance and communal worship.

## Modern application
Public worship, personal devotion and government-sponsored prayer raise different questions. Neither set of texts directly addresses modern constitutional arrangements.

## Verification
Compare prayer terminology, rhetorical context, Gospel parallels and the Mark 11 textual variant. Review scholarly interpretations of Matthew 6 and Pauline assembly practice.
