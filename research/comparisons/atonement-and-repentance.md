## Textual comparison

Jesus forgives sins in Mark 2, describes his life as a ransom in Mark 10 and calls for repentance in Luke 13. Luke 19 narrates restitution and salvation. Paul links redemption and divine righteousness with Christ's death in Romans 3 and describes release from the curse in Galatians 3.

## Competing interpretations and limitations

Paul's account may develop themes already present in Gospel ransom language. The fact that forgiveness occurs before the crucifixion in Gospel narrative does not by itself disprove a later theological account of the death's significance. Conversely, the texts do not explicitly articulate one uniform atonement mechanism.

## Modern implications

Distinguish repentance, restitution, forgiveness and competing atonement theories rather than assuming one model is universal.
