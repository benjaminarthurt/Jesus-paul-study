## Textual comparison
Matthew 10 restricts the initial mission to Israel rather than Gentile or Samaritan destinations. Luke 9 and 10 narrate commissions involving travel, hospitality, proclamation and healing. Matthew 28 presents a post-resurrection commission to all nations. These narrative stages must not be collapsed into one timeless instruction.

Galatians 2 describes Paul's Gentile mission and its recognition by other leaders, alongside conflict over shared meals. Galatians 3 presents unity in Christ across ethnic and social distinctions.

## Competing interpretations
Paul's mission may extend a universal commission already represented in Matthew's narrative. Alternatively, the historical expansion beyond Israel may represent a significant development in the movement, with later Gospel accounts reflecting that development. The literary sequence of Matthew is not independent proof of the historical chronology of every commission.

## Modern application
Mission, cultural assimilation, religious pluralism and ethnic inclusion should be distinguished. Gentile inclusion does not automatically resolve every modern question about conversion or cultural identity.

## Verification
Compare mission vocabulary, Gospel composition and the historical chronology of Galatians. Examine Acts as a separate narrative witness and consult competing historical scholarship.
