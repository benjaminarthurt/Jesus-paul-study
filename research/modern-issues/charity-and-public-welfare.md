## Ancient textual evidence

Jesus challenges attachment to wealth and depicts obligations toward vulnerable people. Paul organizes collections and discusses voluntary giving.

See the [related textual comparison](/comparisons/wealth-and-generosity/) for passage references and competing readings.

## Modern application and boundaries

Private generosity, mutual aid, taxation and public welfare are different mechanisms. The ancient texts provide ethical considerations but do not establish a specific tax rate or welfare model.

## Receiving a meal without incurring a social debt

Luke's meal scene challenges a host's choice of invitees. Paul's rebuke in Corinth concerns people who reach the gathering and still leave hungry or humiliated. For a contemporary community meal, access and treatment therefore deserve separate examination. A congregation could compare its stated invitation with how guests actually learn about the meal, how they enter and how portions are distributed. Asking participants what would make the meal easier to attend supplies evidence the ancient narratives cannot provide. [Hospitality and shared meals](/comparisons/hospitality-and-shared-meals/)

The Didache's rules for short stays and resident teachers show how one early community differentiated forms of support. Applying those limits to public benefits today would require an independent argument connecting a community of hosts with the duties of a state. Eligibility, funding, disability access and the consequences of limiting assistance cannot be settled by the number of days in an ancient rule for travelers.
