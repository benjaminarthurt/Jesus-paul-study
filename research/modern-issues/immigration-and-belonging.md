## Who speaks in the biblical accounts?

In Matthew 2 an angel warns Joseph and the family leaves for Egypt to escape Herod. Joseph responds to a later message by returning, then avoids Judea when he hears who rules there. Matthew narrates decisions made for a threatened child. Jesus gives no instruction in this scene, and Matthew describes no hearing or admission procedure. The narrative can inform how a reader sees a family's fear even where its institutional details offer little guidance. [Flight and return](/passages/mt-02-13-23/)

In Luke the Samaritan pays for a stranger's immediate care and promises to cover further costs. In Matthew 25 the king treats hospitality as service rendered to himself. Readers disagree over whether the king identifies particularly with vulnerable disciples or broadly with people in need. The narrower reading changes the argument one can make from Matthew alone. Luke independently makes the helper's response to injury central. The cited article was available here at abstract level. ## Who already belongs?

Paul's objection at Antioch concerns Gentile believers who had shared meals with Cephas. His account exposes how an informal practice can undo recognition already granted. A congregation that welcomes immigrant members on paper can still exclude them from meals and decisions. A state deciding a protection claim faces a different question. UNHCR distinguishes seeking asylum from the procedure that establishes whether the person's circumstances meet the criteria for refugee status. The person's immediate need for food or shelter can arise well before that determination. ## From a meal to a public decision

A church offering a meal can ask newcomers what makes attendance possible and invite them to organize it. The Cape Town Commitment explicitly addresses immigrant churches as partners and hosts as providers of hospitality. For a municipality deciding how to allocate scarce shelter places, the question extends to people already waiting, the consequences of delay, and fair ways to establish urgency. The Samaritan gives a reason to take injury seriously. Translating that reason into an allocation rule requires evidence about local need and an account of fairness. None of these passages sets a numerical admissions policy. This dossier has not assessed any jurisdiction's procedures or interviewed affected communities.
