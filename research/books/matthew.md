# Gospel of Matthew — first literary-unit and selected sayings pass

**Research state: draft.** The new literary unit map provides continuous reference coverage across all 28 chapters using **119 preliminary units** (including eight existing research anchors). Every verse is assigned to exactly one unit by the automated boundary validator. This **does not** mean every individual saying has been extracted, or that each segment boundary is a scholarly consensus pericope division.

## Primary reference edition

- [World English Bible, Protestant 66-book edition](https://ebible.org/details.php?id=engwebp) (public domain text; edition name is a trademark).
- The website includes precise Matthew chapter/verse references, **original summaries**, and links to the external source chapters, not copied biblical verses.
- Reference bounds follow Matthew's 28 chapters in the website's edition. Verse counts are structural validation inputs; manuscript-level variants are not resolved by arithmetic.
- *Matthew 17:21* has an explicit textual warning based on the WEBP chapter footnote, which says the NU text omits the verse. The catalog includes the verse in its navigation range but does **not** certify its originality.

## Literary arrangement

The narrative is commonly analyzed as five major discourse collections interspersed with narratives: Sermon on the Mount (5–7), mission instructions (10), parables (13), community instructions (18), and eschatological discourse (23–25). The infancy narratives (1–2), ministry narrative and the passion/resurrection accounts (26–28) require their own status rather than being labeled unmediated speech by Jesus.

- [Felix Just, *The Gospel according to Matthew: Five Major Discourses of Jesus*](https://www.catholic-resources.org/Bible/Matthew-Discourses.htm) provides an accessible guide to this organization and provisional cross-Gospel reference ranges. Specific divisions are editorial and remain open to review.
- The inventory uses `speech`, `dialogue`, `parable`, `prayer`, `action`, and `context` kinds. A literary unit can contain framing narration, another speaker, or more than one saying. The first two chapters consist of narrative witnesses, not teachings uttered by Jesus.

## Selected speech-bearing spans

The project now indexes **75 preliminary Matthew speech-bearing spans**, separate from the existing 41 Mark spans. Every selected span points to a parent Matthew unit, and the parent is linked to related research topics. These are *not* literal quotations.

- Candidate relationships connect the Matthew spans to **94 Mark, Luke or John references**. `parallel` indicates a provisional matching episode/saying; `related` indicates a thematic or tradition connection with possible setting or wording differences.
- Candidate references are **not** assertions of independent eyewitness reports, verified word-for-word equivalence, or a solved Synoptic Problem. Where Matthew appears to rework Mark or share materials with Luke, the precise literary relation is a historical debate.
- Matthew's prayer wording, final judgment scene, and resurrection commission carry interpretive or textual cautions. Mark 16's disputed longer ending remains excluded from the original Mark sayings pilot.

## Integrity and limits

- Existing anchors used by earlier Law, Wealth, and Women research comparisons **retain their stable IDs and ranges**.
- A page displaying `Matthew 5:21–48` as one broad literary unit does not imply it consists of one uninterrupted utterance or theme.
- Search and comparison pages now include the Matthew additions as source-labeled reference summaries, with no assumed contradictions generated from cross-Gospel candidate lists.
- Review remains `draft`: no full critical Greek apparatus, independent commentary review, or exhaustive clause-level segmentation has yet been completed.
